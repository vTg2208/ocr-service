## Design Context

### Users

Ministry, state, district, forest, revenue, and planning officials use this interface to understand FRA progress, resolve review gaps, inspect spatial records, and coordinate development interventions. Registry and verification staff use it for detailed casework, while programme leaders need a calm overview that identifies where attention is required without obscuring evidence quality.

### Brand Personality

Authoritative, calm, and precise. The interface should create confidence in the record-handling process without feeling bureaucratic, technical, or decorative.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light institutional interface with a restrained forest-and-land character, emblem-led forest green, green-tinted neutral surfaces, clear data hierarchy, and practical density. Maps and evidence should feel integral to the FRA programme rather than decorative. Amber and red are reserved for warnings and errors. Avoid generic AI-dashboard patterns, Patta-led framing, oversized decorative typography, excessive monospace text, technical implementation language, redundant cards, and unnecessary explanatory copy.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the programme condition and the few issues requiring human attention.
2. Connect every summary to its underlying FRA cases, geography, evidence, and provenance.
3. Reveal detailed counts and breakdowns progressively instead of presenting every value at once.
4. Communicate legal status, data completeness, and advisory results through clear language, shape, and color rather than color alone.
5. Optimize detailed workspaces for repeated staff use while keeping the overview legible to senior decision-makers.
6. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, comfortable touch targets, and responsive layouts.
